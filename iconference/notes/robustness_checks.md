# Robustness and sanity checks — lexical distinctiveness analysis

These checks accompany `scripts/02_lexical_distinctiveness.py` and Figure 2 /
`tables/table_lexical_distinctive_terms.csv`. All numbers below are produced
by that script and saved under `derived/`; nothing here was hand-edited.

## 1. Sensitivity to the minimum document-frequency threshold

`derived/lexical_sensitivity_mindf.csv` reports vocabulary size at three
plausible thresholds:

| min_df | terms passing threshold |
|---|---|
| 3  | 12,264 |
| 5 (main analysis) | 6,322 |
| 10 | 3,256 |

`derived/lexical_sensitivity_top15_overlap.csv` compares the top-15
strongest terms per side at min_df=5 (the analysis reported in the paper)
against min_df=3 and min_df=10:

| Comparison | Top-15 pre-2023 overlap | Top-15 2024-2025 overlap |
|---|---|---|
| min_df=5 vs. min_df=3  | 0.93 | 1.00 |
| min_df=5 vs. min_df=10 | 0.87 | 1.00 |

**Interpretation:** the 2024-2025-associated top terms are identical
regardless of threshold (these are the highest-frequency terms in the
corpus, e.g. "generative", "genai", "chatgpt", so they are unaffected by
min_df choice at any of these three levels). The pre-2023-associated top
terms are 87-93% stable, with 1-2 lower-frequency terms swapping in or out
near the threshold boundary. The main qualitative story (a shift from
conceptual/curricular/child-oriented vocabulary toward generative-AI-tool
and critical-evaluation vocabulary) is unaffected by the min_df choice.

## 2. Are results dominated by bare technology names?

`derived/lexical_tech_term_audit.csv` and
`derived/lexical_tech_term_audit_summary.csv` flag whether each of the
top-15 terms per side matches a known generative-AI product/technology
name (chatgpt, gpt, llm(s), genai, generative, gemini, bard, copilot,
claude, openai, llama, dall-e, midjourney, bing).

| Side | Tech-name terms | Total top terms | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-2023 | 0 | 15 | 0.00 |
| 2024-2025 | 3 | 15 | 0.20 |

**Interpretation:** only 3 of the 15 strongest 2024-2025 terms
("generative", "genai", "chatgpt") are bare technology names; the
remaining 12 (e.g. "academic", "integration", "higher", "critical
thinking", "engagement", "acceptance", "nursing", "tools") describe
institutional, pedagogical, or evaluative concerns rather than naming a
specific tool. The lexical shift is not a pure artifact of the corpus now
containing more mentions of "ChatGPT"; it also reflects a shift in the
*surrounding* conceptual vocabulary (institutional integration, critical
thinking, professional acceptance).

## 3. Inspecting underlying documents

`derived/lexical_example_documents.csv` lists up to 3 example document
titles per top-5 term on each side (25-30 rows total), generated by a
simple case-insensitive whole-word match of the term against `topic_text`.
Spot-checking these confirms the terms are used in context as expected —
e.g., pre-2023 "children"/"curriculum"/"learn" examples are about
introducing AI/ML concepts to K-12 students and families; 2024-2025
"generative"/"genai"/"chatgpt" examples are about generative-AI tool use,
integration, and critical evaluation in higher education and academic
writing. No example inspected showed a spurious or out-of-context match.

## 4. Prior-strength sensitivity, document prevalence, and literal bigrams
   (added in the methodological refinement pass)

Three further checks were added after the checks above and are documented
in their own notes rather than repeated here in full:

- **Prior-strength sensitivity** — `notes/lexical_prior_sensitivity.md` /
  `tables/table_lexical_prior_sensitivity_summary.csv`. Tests alpha0 at
  0.25x/0.50x/1.00x/2.00x the smaller period's token count. Result: robust
  (Spearman rho ≥ 0.999 vs. the current 1.00x specification at every
  setting tested; top-15 overlap ≥ 93%).
- **Document prevalence** — `tables/table_lexical_distinctive_terms_with_docprevalence.csv`.
  Adds, for every term in the top-30 table, the number and percentage of
  each period's documents that actually contain the term (not just token
  counts). Most top terms are widespread (e.g. "machine" in 25.5% of
  pre-2023 documents); a handful of pre-2023 terms are driven by very few
  documents ("book," "e-learning," "intention learn" each in ≤3 of 106
  pre-2023 documents) and should be read cautiously.
- **Literal contiguous bigram check** — `notes/literal_bigram_check.md` /
  `derived/lexical_literal_bigram_check.csv`. Directly tests the "false
  adjacency" limitation noted below by re-deriving bigrams as literally
  adjacent word pairs. All three bigrams in the main top-30 table survive
  and rank 1st-2nd by this alternative construction; several additional
  interpretable multiword expressions also emerge, consistent with the
  main analysis's substantive story.

## 5. Known limitations (see also iconference/README.md)

- **Self-referential Dirichlet prior shape.** No external/independent
  background corpus was available in this repository, so the prior's
  relative-frequency shape is estimated from the same two-period corpus
  being compared (see method note in `02_lexical_distinctiveness.py` and
  in the main README). The prior's total mass (alpha0) is capped at the
  smaller period's token count specifically to avoid the corpus dominating
  its own prior; an earlier version that set alpha0 to the full combined
  corpus size produced implausibly compressed z-scores for even the most
  obviously skewed terms (e.g., "generative": 5 pre-2023 vs. 1,307
  2024-2025 occurrences yielded |z| < 2), confirming that choice was
  degenerate. The capped version produces z-scores whose ranking is stable
  under the min_df sensitivity check above.
- **Naive bigram adjacency after stopword removal.** Bigrams are formed
  from the token stream *after* stopword/noise-token removal (a standard,
  documented simplification also used in the parent project's BERTopic
  vectorizer), which can occasionally create a bigram between two words
  that were not literally adjacent in the original sentence. Given the
  min_df=5 threshold and the manual document inspection in section 3
  above, this did not visibly affect the reported top terms. **This was
  directly tested** in the literal contiguous bigram check (section 4
  above): all three bigrams in the main top-30 table are confirmed as
  genuine literal adjacent phrases.
- **Class imbalance.** Pre-2023 (106 documents) is far smaller than
  2024-2025 (1,867 documents). The Monroe et al. z-score is designed to
  account for this via per-period variance weighting, and the sensitivity
  and tech-name checks above did not surface signs that the imbalance is
  driving spurious results, but readers should treat pre-2023 estimates as
  based on a comparatively small sample.
